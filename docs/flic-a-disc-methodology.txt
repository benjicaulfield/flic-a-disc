FLIC-A-DISC OPERATING METHODOLOGY
==================================
A Personal Record Buying & Debt Reduction Framework


CORE TENETS
-----------
1. Only handle records you love.
2. Don't spend any money (the operation is self-funded).
3. You can walk away at any time with no negative consequences.

These three tenets are mutually reinforcing. Loving the records you handle
gives you the market knowledge to price and buy accurately. Self-funding
means no external obligation. The exit condition means no trapped inventory.


THE MARGIN TARGET
-----------------
Target margin: 40%
Current realized margin: ~27%, trending upward

All buying decisions are filtered through this margin target at the point
of purchase — not calculated after the fact. The margin is the buying filter.

Example math (Discogs, ~11% seller fees):
  Sale price:          $15.00
  Less Discogs fees:   - $1.65  (~11%)
  Net proceeds:        $13.35
  40% margin target:   buy price no more than $8.00

At current average sale price ($15) and volume (100 records/month), the
gap between 27% and 40% is approximately $2 per record. Closing this
gap is the primary operational lever.


SOURCING STRATEGY
-----------------
Primary channel: Discogs, foreign sellers with free shipping thresholds.

This is the core channel and where the model focuses most of its energy.
Supply is not a constraint — the limiting variables are buying discipline
and how accurately the model identifies the spread between a foreign
seller's ask and Discogs market value.

Why this works:
- No buyer fees on Discogs.
- Free shipping threshold eliminates shipping cost from the buy side,
  directly widening margin without needing to find a cheaper record.
- Foreign sellers are often underpriced relative to Discogs market value,
  creating the spread that generates margin.
- The free shipping subsidy from the seller is effectively free money
  that goes straight to your spread — making this the highest-margin
  channel available.

The Flic-a-Disc ML model amplifies this strategy by identifying records
where the spread between what a seller is asking and what the market
will bear is widest — underpriced listings, overlooked pressings, sellers
who don't know what they have.


BUYING CHANNELS & CARD ASSIGNMENTS
------------------------------------
Each credit card is assigned a buying channel. This creates clean
accounting, makes per-channel ROI visible, and aligns cashback rewards
with spending categories.

  Card 1 (lowest APR / best cashback):  Discogs overseas (primary channel)
  Card 2:                                eBay Buy It Now
  Card 3:                                eBay Auctions
  Card 4:                                Record stores (in-person retail)
  PayPal balance:                        CL / FB Marketplace cash deals

Selling income comes in via PayPal. Physical buying cash stays in
PayPal until needed (liquid but buffered from daily spending).

Note: Airline points cards are lower priority than cashback cards.
A 2-3% cashback is a guaranteed return. Airline points yield ~1-1.5
cents per dollar and require redemption to realize value. During a
debt paydown phase, cashback wins.


THE MARGIN SPLIT
----------------
Of every dollar of margin realized:
  50% reinvested into purchasing
  50% directed to credit card paydown

At current volume and margin:
  $1,500/month sales × 27% margin = ~$405 margin
  ~$200 to purchasing, ~$200 to cards

At target margin:
  $1,500/month sales × 40% margin = ~$600 margin
  ~$300 to purchasing, ~$300 to cards

The reinvestment half compounds the operation over time. The paydown
half chips away at debt. Both grow as margin improves.


CASH FLOW STRUCTURE
-------------------
All selling income:     → PayPal (holding account)
PayPal surplus:         → Personal checking (1-3 day ACH)
From personal checking: → Card payments (avalanche method)
Card purchases:         → Categorized by buying channel (see above)

PayPal functions as a behavioral buffer — keeping liquid cash out of
the personal checking account reduces the likelihood it gets spent
before being allocated. CL/FB Marketplace cash needs to stay liquid
and accessible for quick in-person deals without waiting on ACH.


DEBT PAYDOWN METHOD
-------------------
Method: Avalanche (highest APR first)

Pay minimums on all cards automatically. Direct the 50% margin
allocation to the highest-APR card as an additional payment each month.
As that balance drops, minimum payments drop slightly, freeing
incrementally more to accelerate paydown.

Milestones to target:
  1. Get each card below its credit limit (eliminates over-limit risk,
     begins restoring credit utilization).
  2. Pay off highest-APR card fully.
  3. Redirect that card's freed minimum payment to the next card.


THE COMPOUNDING EFFECT
----------------------
Better buying discipline
  → Higher margin per record
    → More purchasing power (50% reinvested)
      → More records sold
        → More total margin
          → More to cards
            → Lower balances
              → Lower minimums
                → More surplus available

The system is slow to start but self-reinforcing. The margin trend
(27% → 40%) is the leading indicator. Everything else follows from it.


FLIC-A-DISC MODEL ROLE
----------------------
The ML system is a neural contextual bandit with contrastive encoding,
trained on scraped Discogs and eBay listings and annotated through
active use. It serves the methodology in two ways:

1. Taste filter: Surfaces records worth handling — aligned with what
   you love and what you know well enough to price accurately.

2. Margin signal: Because loving a record correlates with knowing its
   market, high-confidence model picks are also likely to be records
   where the spread between ask and realized market price is widest.
   Underpriced records you recognize; overpriced records you pass on.

The bandit's exploration-exploitation tradeoff maps directly onto
buying strategy. Exploitation means buying records the model
confidently predicts will hit 40% margin. Exploration means
occasionally buying outside that zone to gather new signal.

As the model improves, it increasingly identifies the specific foreign
listings where the margin gap is largest — which is where the path
from 27% to 40% lives. Closing that gap is the primary optimization
target.


PLATFORM EXPANSION (FLIC-A-DISC MARKETPLACE)
---------------------------------------------
The longer-term vision is opening flic-a-disc.com as a co-op
marketplace for like-minded sellers who want an alternative to
Discogs' 11.2% fee structure.

Stack: React/TS frontend, Go backend, Python/Django ML layer,
PostgreSQL, hosted on DigitalOcean.

Fee model: Cost-plus, transparent, recalculated on a 4-week
rolling window.

  Per transaction costs:
    Processing (Helcim):   1.79% + $0.08
    Overhead (flat):       Total overhead ÷ transaction count

  Overhead pool includes:
    DigitalOcean hosting + operator stipend + any tooling costs

  Example at 1,000 transactions/month, $50 avg, $600 overhead:
    Helcim:    ~$0.975/transaction
    Overhead:  ~$0.60/transaction
    Total:     ~$1.575/transaction = ~3.15% effective rate
    vs Discogs: 11.2% = $5.60/transaction

  Seller savings at this volume: ~$4.03 per transaction

As transaction volume grows the overhead component divides across
more transactions, so the effective rate decreases automatically.
Sellers have direct incentive to recruit other sellers.

Target for $2,000/month stipend at or below 3% effective rate:
  ~4,000 transactions/month across ~40 active sellers
  at $50 average transaction value = $200,000 gross monthly volume

Shipping is passed through at actual cost, seller-defined per
transaction. It is not a platform revenue source and flows directly
to the seller, eliminating the fee-circumvention dynamic common
on other platforms.


SUMMARY
-------
Buy only records you love, from foreign Discogs sellers with free
shipping, at prices that leave at least 40% margin after fees. Split
that margin 50/50 between reinvestment and card paydown. Route all
cash through PayPal as a behavioral buffer. Pay cards via avalanche.
Let the model get smarter. Let the margin compound. Build the platform.
