NORTH STAR

Discogs makes money on an 11.2% fee. The methodology's thesis is that loving a record correlates with knowing its market, so high-confidence model picks are also the widest ask-vs-market spreads. Every line of this sprint ladders up to one sentence in the cover letter: "I built a marketplace, a daily buy-recommendation pipeline, and a margin-aware recommender, and here are the numbers."

The buying decision formula. Max bid = net_proceeds * (1 - required_margin) - shipping, where net_proceeds = sell_price * 0.85, shipping = $6 flat, and required_margin slides from 50% at low prices to 15% at high prices via: margin = 0.15 + 0.35 * exp(-0.012 * sell_price). This is the number displayed on every recommendation.

The classifier story. You now have 50000 labeled eBay listings from real scrapes, roughly 2% keepers. This is the actual training data. TF-IDF similarity against a Discogs corpus was the stopgap. A logistic regression on bigram title features plus price, trained on these real labels, is the real Stage 2 filter. It gets trained on Day 1 and retrained nightly from then on. Every day the tools run, the model gets slightly better automatically. Annotation and model improvement are the same activity.

RULES FOR THIS SPRINT

Annotate while using. Every tab that shows records has a way to mark keepers. Never do a big annotation drudge session — let the daily usage accumulate signal naturally.

Retrain nightly from Day 5. Even if the model is mediocre on Day 5, it should be retraining every night. Mediocre-and-improving beats perfect-and-static.

No new scrape types or data sources until the marketplace is done. The three scrape types you have (auctions, BIN, best match) are enough. Resist the urge to add more data collection before the pipeline and marketplace are solid.

One commit per day minimum. End every day with a commit and one sentence for the case study. The case study assembles itself from 30 sentences.

Protect the three checkpoints: Day 7 (pipeline running), Day 19 (marketplace demo-able), Day 30 (shipped). If a day slips, carry the task and protect the checkpoint. The 70% rule: 70% done on time beats 100% done late.

The marketplace week is the biggest risk. If Helcim integration stalls, the fee engine plus order lifecycle plus transparency dashboard with a mock provider is still a complete story. The economics and transparency are the point. Protect the dashboard — that is the screenshot that gets you the interview.
